Maps: minimap and full-screen map

A Waypoint Map is a picture of your level from above. The radar shows it as a minimap, and M opens it as a full-screen map with the player and every waypoint on it. Waypoints and the player move live on the map; the picture itself is made once, in the editor (it is not a live camera view).

Add one #

  • Setup Wizard step 3: Add Map And Make It, or
  • GameObject > Bearing > Waypoint Map, then Fit To Scene and Make Clean Map.

The blue box in the Scene view is the area the map covers. Its top edge is north (the world's forward, Z).

Three kinds of picture #

Picture from What it is Best for
Clean map A clean, flat map drawn from your level's shapes: roads, paths, grass, sand, water, rock, buildings and forest, with relief shading and outlines. No camera, so it is the same in Built-in, URP and HDRP. Most 3D games
Photo from above A picture taken by a camera looking straight down. 2D games, or levels the Clean map can't read
My own picture Any top-down picture you painted, north at the top. Hand-made treasure maps, stylised games

Making a Clean map #

  1. Press Preview. The map is drawn in the inspector without saving anything.
  2. Anything shown in pink wasn't recognised. It's listed under the preview: pick what each one is (Road, Building, Water, Leave off the map...) and press Use. A rule is added and the preview is drawn again.
  3. Press Make Clean Map. The picture is saved in Assets/RareGeometryData/Bearing/Maps.

How it decides what things are, first match wins:

  1. A Waypoint Map Category component on the object or a parent (add it to anything to say exactly what it is).
  2. Your rules (Colours, rules and finishing): words in a material name, object name, layer, tag or Terrain layer.
  3. The built-in words: road, street, asphalt; path, pavement, plaza, bridge; grass, lawn, field; sand, beach; water, sea, lake, river; rock, cliff, mountain; house, building, shop, tower, tent; tree, forest, pine...
  4. Small things (under 1.5 m across) are left off: props, crates, plants, characters.
  5. A hint from the material's colour (green is grass, blue is water).

Terrains are drawn from their Terrain layers (named like grass, sand, road, rock...) and their painted trees become forest. Height range draws one floor of a multi-level place.

Setting What it does
Picture size 64 to 4096 pixels on the longer side. Bigger is sharper but uses more memory; 1024 or 2048 is usually plenty.
Layers to draw Leave out the player, effects and UI.
Unknown things become Left off (default), or drawn as ground.
Relief, outlines Slope shading lit from the north-west; edges round buildings, roads and water.
Colours One colour per kind of place, and the empty colour (sea, void).

The full-screen map #

M (or hud.ToggleMap()) opens it. Drag to move, wheel or + / - to zoom, Me or Space to centre on the player, M or Escape to close. It shows the player arrow, every waypoint with its name, discovered region names, fog of war and a legend for Clean maps. On the HUD you choose the key, whether the mouse is freed (default) and whether the game pauses while it's open. Its look (colours, sizes, words) is in the style's Full-screen map section.

Coordinate grid. Full-screen map > Grid draws columns A, B, C... and rows 1, 2, 3... over the map (square cells; Grid Columns sets how many across, Grid Color the lines), for strategy and survival games and for team callouts ("enemy at C4"). Off by default.

Your own marker. Click anywhere on the map to place a marker there; click it again to remove it, or click somewhere else to move it. It is an ordinary waypoint, so it shows on the screen, the compass and the minimap too, and WaypointSave saves it. On level maps it lands on the ground below the point. Turn it off with Players can place a marker on the HUD; from code: WaypointPlayerPin.Place(position), PlaceOnMap(map, uv, height), Clear(), Current, and the Changed event.

Touch (phones and tablets): drag with one finger, pinch with two to zoom about the point between them, tap a place for its card (a finger gets a larger target than the mouse). It uses UI Toolkit's own touch events, so it needs no setup and the mouse and gamepad work as before.

Gamepad (with the Input System package): View/Select opens and closes the map, the left stick moves it under a crosshair, the triggers zoom, A (Cross) places or removes your marker at the crosshair, Y (Triangle) centres on you and B (Circle) closes. Turn it off with Gamepad controls the map. Your own input code can drive it instead by setting BearingInput.PadOverride, or call hud.ToggleMap(), hud.ZoomMap(factor) and hud.PanMap(delta). While the map is open, WaypointHud.IsAnyMapOpen is true: ignore gameplay input then (the demo's controller does).

Search #

Players press F (or the Search button on the controls bar) and type part of a place's name or its category. Up and Down choose, Enter goes: the map zooms in enough for the place to show, turns to its floor, centres on it and opens its card. Only places the player knows are found, never an undiscovered place's name. The key and an off switch are on the HUD (Full-screen map); the words are in the style's map controls. From code: hud.SearchMap("mill"), hud.MapSearchResults, hud.PickSearchResult().

Zoom detail #

Zoomed out, small places (chests, collectibles) can wait for the player to zoom in: the style's Full-screen map > Zoom Detail sets from which zoom each category shows. The objective, tracked places and the player's own marks always show. The Chart Table's Map tab shows what appears zoomed out, half way and zoomed in.

Why not a live camera map? #

A live minimap renders the level a second time every frame through an extra camera, which works differently in each render pipeline and costs performance. A made picture looks the same everywhere, costs nothing, and never breaks with a Unity or pipeline update. If your level changes a lot while playing, make a few pictures and swap WaypointMap.Picture.

The map explains itself #

  • Controls bar along the bottom: move, zoom, centre, place a marker, fast travel (when a travel point is on the map), legend and close, each with its key, and with gamepad buttons once a gamepad is used (Glyphs: Auto, Keyboard, Gamepad, Both). Click Zoom, Centre, Legend or Close on the bar to do it.
  • Place cards: point at a place (or move the gamepad crosshair onto it) to see its name, state, category and distance, with a Fast travel button when the place allows it. The card sits beside the place, never on it.
  • First-open hint: a line at the top the first time the map opens in a session (or every time).
  • L hides or shows the legends.

All of it is in the style: Full-screen map > Controls (on/off for each part, the words, the key and button names to match your own bindings, colours, hint time).

Bridges #

The Clean map draws bridges, piers, jetties, boardwalks and docks (by object or material name, or a Map Category of Bridge) over the water and paths they cross, whatever order things are drawn in, with a darker edge. Colour: Clean > Bridge.

Buildings, caves and sections: map areas #

A map can be the map of one building, cave, district or dungeon section. Add a Waypoint Map to it, size its box to the building and tick Is Area (Waypoint Map > Area and floors). While the player is inside the box (and the Area Height range), the minimap and the full-screen map show the area's map; walking out goes back to the map around it.

Setting What it does
Area Name The name on the maps (the object's name when empty).
Area Height The heights the area covers (both 0 = any height): a cellar under a house is its own area.
Priority Where areas overlap, the higher priority wins (a room in a building in a district).
Auto Switch Switch by itself when the player walks in (off: only from code).
Transition, Transition Time How the minimap changes: fade or none.
On Parent How the area shows on the map around it: outline, icon, its own picture, or nothing. Click it on the full-screen map to look inside.

On the full-screen map, the button at the top left steps out to the map around an area and back in. Each HUD picks its own map, so split-screen players in different buildings each see their own. From code: hud.ActiveMap, hud.ViewedMap, hud.ViewMap(map), hud.ViewParentMap(), hud.MapChanged, WaypointMap.For(position). Areas work across scenes loaded together (each is found by its scene and name: keep area names different).

Floors #

Fill Floors with a name and the heights of each floor. Bake All Floors draws each floor from its own heights only, so the roof and the floors above are cut away, and sets each floor's picture (Clean maps and photos both).

  • The player's floor follows their height, with Floor Hysteresis metres to spare, so stairs and jumps never make it
  • Put the boundary between two floors about halfway up the stairs, not just under the upper floor: the player's height is measured at their feet, and a player standing on the upper floor must be more than Floor Hysteresis above the boundary to count as upstairs. flicker. hud.PlayerFloor, hud.FloorChanged.
  • The full-screen map opens on the player's floor; the arrows step through floors. When the objective is on another floor, the floor name says which. Browse Unvisited Floors off: only floors the player has been on can be picked.
  • Other Floor Places: places on other floors are dimmed (the minimap adds up and down arrows), or hidden.
  • Fog Per Floor: each floor has its own fog of war.
  • Saved: visited floors and each floor's fog (save format 5).

Diagnostics warns about floors without a picture (with a Bake All Floors button), places outside every floor's heights, and overlapping areas with the same priority. The Chart Table's Places list shows each place's area and lets you pick its floor by name.
